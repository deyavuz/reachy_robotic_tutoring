## Notes

**Multiple-choice items.** Several CPR items (2, 4, 6, 7, 9b, 10, 15) offer
options. Decide whether the participant reads the options aloud or only the
stem. Reading them makes the item easier and changes what the calibration
figures mean; leaving them out makes it open-ended. **Be consistent, and say
which you chose in your methods.**

**Item 15 needs the sequence.** The participant has to read out the fifteen
odd/even results. Consider putting them on screen as a list rather than prose.

**KC4 has only two items.** The CPR test contains exactly one pure total-probability
item (14); Item 5 is the nearest relative, handing you the marginal and the joint
and asking for the conditional. There is nothing else in the instrument. Two is
still better than one, which would make KC4 a single-shot gate.

**Dropped: the Gigerenzer & Hoffrage (1995) mammography item.** It and CPR Item 5
are the same Eddy 1982 scenario in different formats — both come out near 7.8%.
Using both would look repetitive to a participant. CPR Item 18 (two machines)
takes the KC5 slot instead, and is the strongest item in the instrument.

**Q11 adapted from CPR Item 5.** The original item's phrasing (Eddy 1982's
80% sensitivity / 9.6% false-positive rate, answer ~7.8%) omitted those two
figures from the participant-facing text, relying on the "closer to 8% or 80%"
forced choice instead of a computable stem. The version now in use states
sensitivity (90%) and false-positive rate (9%) explicitly so the question is
self-contained, giving an answer of ~9.2%. The 37 → 48 before/after figure is
carried over from the original CPR item and has not been re-measured for the
adapted numbers.

**If you want a 15th item:** CPR Item 3 (cards, independence versus mutual
exclusivity, 23 → 41) fits KC1, or Item 13 (dice, product is 12, 20 → 35)
fits KC2.
